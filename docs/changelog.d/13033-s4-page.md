- **card#13033** — **Every floor can now be walked in 3-D, at `/floor/{floor}/3d`, beside its 2-D page: turn round it, tilt from straight down to just above the floor, slide across it and zoom in, with every desk, character, piece of furniture and label in place.** The floor page's *Floor in 3D* pill opens it and its *2D* pill returns; the 3-D page is the floor page itself — the same status strip, panel, list view and URLs, a desk's drill-down at `/floor/{floor}/3d/{seat}` — over a Three.js renderer (`server/public/js/floor3d/floor-paint.js`). Left-drag or one finger turns, right-drag or two fingers slide along the floor, the wheel or a pinch zooms (and scrolls the page once the camera can go no closer or farther), the arrows turn and tilt, Shift+arrows slide, `+`/`−` zoom and *Fit the floor* returns to the opening view; the camera never goes below the floor and never leaves it, and a wall or the back wall between the camera and what it looks at is lowered to its foot. The desks' furniture and the scenery are Kenney's Furniture Kit repainted in the floor's theme, and the pieces the kit lacks (the restroom door, water cooler, filing cabinet, copier, whiteboard, aquarium, bean bag and globe) are drawn by the house theme's new `piece3d()`. Each monitor faces its sitter and each laptop its intern, and every desk's status floats over it as a label readable from any side; labels that would overlap are faded, never removed, so a screen reader hears every desk in reading order. The scene draws only while something moves. A browser without WebGL 2 is shown a statement with a link to the 2-D page, and the list of desks. No seat can be named `3d` any more: the console and `mezzanine:ingest-token:issue` refuse that id, because it is the 3-D floor's address. Interns' 3-D bodies are built with fewer facets. `tools/design/floor3d.browser.mjs` draws the page in a real browser and checks the camera, the URL, the labels, the frame loop and § 6's draw-call, triangle and texture budgets at the cap; `--out <dir>` saves pictures of it.
